\section{The Topological Funnel and Open Problems}

Theorem~\ref{thm:classification} reveals a striking geometric structure in the
space of achievable defect values. At the sparse limit ($D = R-1$), the number
of valid connected topologies is the number of non-isomorphic trees on $R$
vertices, which grows exponentially (OEIS A000055). As the defect increases, the
geometric constraints imposed by root-sharing progressively tighten the design
space, creating a \emph{Topological Funnel} that narrows monotonically until it
is conjectured to collapse to a single topology---the Hamming Ball---at the
dense limit $D = \Eseq(R)$. At powers of $2$, the funnel exhibits a discrete
fracture: the gap
$\gamma(R) = d - 1$ excises an entire band of defect values, forcing a
discontinuous jump from the second-best topology to the unique optimum. Similar
secondary fractures emerge just below near-powers of $2$ (e.g., $R=2^d-1$).

\begin{conjecture}[Multiplicity of Achievable Defects]\label{conj:multiplicity}
For each $R$ and each achievable defect $D \in \mathcal{D}(R)$, let $M(R, D)$
denote the number of non-isomorphic connected $R$-vertex subgraphs of $A(n,k)$
with defect exactly $D$. Then:
	\begin{enumerate}
\item $M(R, \Eseq(R)) = 1$ for all $R \ge 2$ (the Hamming Ball is the unique
maximizer).
\item $M(R, D)$ is non-decreasing as $D$ decreases from $\Eseq(R)$ toward $R-1$.
	\end{enumerate}
\end{conjecture}

Our exhaustive enumeration confirms both parts for all $R \le 10$.

\paragraph{The openness of Proposition~\ref{prop:universal} is not a
formality.} It is tempting to read Proposition~\ref{prop:universal} as a
routine generalization of the vertex-isoperimetric compression theorems it
invokes, closed by the same techniques with more bookkeeping. Attempts
recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:alt-strategies} show this is not the case.
Every induction skeleton tried so far --- marginal peeling, coordinate-partition
splitting --- fails on an explicit small counterexample, not merely a bound
that is too weak: in $A(5,3)$, splitting a 4-vertex configuration into two
swap-pair fibers $F_a, F_b$ produces $8$ genuinely shared external neighbors
against a $6$-unit recombination budget, and no refinement of the
shared-neighbor count itself can close that gap, because the same
configuration realizes the sharpest $(c_a,c_b,n-k)$-bound on shared neighbors
exactly. The bound survives there only because $F_a$ and $F_b$ each carry
banked boundary slack of their own ($3$ apiece) that happens to more than
cover the $2$-unit deficit --- an amortized, not a local, phenomenon. Whether
every possible split of every configuration inherits enough such slack to
cover its own recombination penalty is exactly as open as
Proposition~\ref{prop:universal} itself; it is, in fact, one precise
restatement of it. This is what is meant by calling the proposition open
rather than merely unproven-for-now: the obstruction is a genuine
global/local tension in the fiber structure, not a gap expected to close with
a longer computation or a sharper application of known compression results.
