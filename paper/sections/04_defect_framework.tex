\section{The Algebraic Defect Framework}\label{sec:defect}

The failure of geometric compression reveals a fundamental constraint: any
method that \emph{moves} symbols between vertices risks violating the
injectivity requirement of the arrangement graph. The algebraic defect framework
circumvents this trap entirely by abandoning vertex-level transformations in
favor of \emph{coordinate-level counting}. Rather than asking ``can we shift
vertex $v$ to reduce the boundary?'' we ask ``how many distinct projections
(roots) does $V'$ cast along each coordinate?'' This root-counting perspective
is immune to the injectivity constraint because it never attempts to reassign
symbols---it merely observes the fiber structure that the set $V'$ already
induces.

Concretely, let $\mathcal R(V')$ be the set of global coordinate roots
(coordinate-aligned cliques) meeting $V'$, and write
$U(V')=|\mathcal R(V')|$.  Each root is a clique of size $n-k+1$.  Every
vertex of $V'$ belongs to exactly one root in each of the $k$ coordinate
directions, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root-membership}
 \sum_{\rho\in\mathcal R(V')}|V'\cap\rho|=k|V'|.
\end{equation}
The defect is $D(V')=k|V'|-U(V')$, as defined in
Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries}.

The framework utilizes a double-counting correction. By counting the available
alphabet symbols, every unique root $r \in U_p(V')$ can be extended to exactly
$(n - k + 1)$ valid vertices in the graph. Subtracting the internal fibers
mapping into $V'$ yields the strictly external coordinate edges
$E_{\mathrm{cand}}$:
\begin{align}\label{eq:ecand}
	E_{\mathrm{cand}} & = \sum_{p=1}^k \Big( |U_p(V')| \cdot (n - k + 1) \Big) - |V'| \cdot k \nonumber                           \\
	                  & = \left(\sum_{p=1}^k |U_p(V')|\right)(n-k) - \left(|V'| \cdot k - \sum_{p=1}^k |U_p(V')|\right) \nonumber \\
	                  & = U(V') \cdot (n-k) - D(V').
\end{align}

By \eqref{eq:root-membership}, the first line of \eqref{eq:ecand} is also
\[
 \sum_{\rho\in\mathcal R(V')}
 \bigl((n-k+1)-|V'\cap\rho|\bigr),
\]
the number of root--external-vertex incidences.  Because external neighbors
may be reached through multiple \emph{coordinate
roots}, we subtract the \emph{cross-collision} mass $X(V')$ to retrieve the
exact boundary of unique vertices. For $w\in\extbd(V')$, let
\[\mu_{V'}(w)=\bigl|\{p\in[k]:\text{the coordinate-}p
\text{ root through }w\text{ is occupied by }V'\}\bigr|.\]
Thus $\mu_{V'}(w)$ counts distinct coordinate directions, not the number of
adjacent members of $V'$: several members can occupy the same root clique.
We define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cross-collision}
X(V') = \sum_{w \in \extbd(V')} (\mu_{V'}(w) - 1).
\end{equation}

Counting those same incidences at their external endpoint gives
\[E_{\mathrm{cand}}
=\sum_{w\in\extbd(V')}\mu_{V'}(w)
=|\extbd(V')|+X(V').\]

The exact boundary size is then:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary}
	\left| \extbd(V') \right| = U(V') \cdot (n - k) - \big(X(V') + D(V')\big).
\end{equation}
The following identities record the exact cost of a coordinate split;
they do not assert that its arithmetic allowance covers that cost.
For a set $V$, fix $p\in[k]$ and let
$F_{p,s}=\{v\in V:v_p=s\}$, retaining only nonempty fibers.
All child quantities are evaluated in the same ambient graph $A(n,k)$.
Write $\Delta D(V,p)=D(V)-\sum_s D(F_{p,s})$ and
$\Delta X(V,p)=X(V)-\sum_s X(F_{p,s})$.

\begin{lemma}[Exact coordinate defect increment]
\label{lem:exact_defect_increment}
For every coordinate split,
\[
 \Delta D(V,p)=|V|-|U_p(V)|.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Deleting coordinate $p$ is injective on each fixed-symbol fiber, so
$|U_p(F_{p,s})|=|F_{p,s}|$.  For $q\ne p$, deleting $q$ retains
the symbol at $p$; consequently roots from different fibers are disjoint and
$|U_q(V)|=\sum_s|U_q(F_{p,s})|$.  Hence
\[
 \sum_s U(F_{p,s})=|V|+\sum_{q\ne p}|U_q(V)|.
\]
Subtracting the defects $D(A)=k|A|-U(A)$ proves the identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact coordinate collision increment]
\label{lem:exact_collision_increment}
Let $\partial_p V$ be the external neighbors of $V$ reached by changing
coordinate $p$.  Then
\[
 \Delta X(V,p)=
 \bigl|\{w\in\extbd(V):\mu_V(w)\ge2,
                  \ w\in\partial_p V\}\bigr|.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $(a)_+=\max\{a,0\}$ and assign multiplicity zero to vertices
internal to a set.  First consider $w\in V$ that is external to a child
$F_{p,s}$.  Since $w_p\ne s$, every adjacency from that child to $w$
changes $p$.  Its child multiplicity is therefore at most one, so
such sibling-internal vertices contribute zero to every child collision count.

Now fix $w\notin V$.  Every generator along $q\ne p$ belongs to
$F_{p,w_p}$, whereas generators along $p$ belong to fibers with
$s\ne w_p$.  Each latter fiber can reach $w$ only along $p$, and thus
contributes no collision excess.  The former fiber has multiplicity
$\mu_V(w)-\mathbf{1}_{\{w\in\partial_p V\}}$; an absent fiber has
multiplicity zero.  Consequently
\[
 \sum_s(\mu_{F_{p,s}}(w)-1)_+
 =\bigl(\mu_V(w)-1-\mathbf{1}_{\{w\in\partial_p V\}}\bigr)_+.
\]
Subtracting this from $(\mu_V(w)-1)_+$ gives one precisely when
$\mu_V(w)\ge2$ and $w\in\partial_p V$, and zero otherwise.
Summing over parent-external vertices, with the sibling contributions
already accounted for, proves the claim.
\end{proof}

In particular, summing over all coordinates gives the exact identity
\[
 \sum_{p=1}^k\Delta X(V,p)
 =\sum_{\substack{w\in\extbd(V)\\\mu_V(w)\ge2}}\mu_V(w).
\]
These are accounting identities, not an independent proof of the universal
boundary inequality below.

The algebraic engine of this framework relies on a fundamental subadditivity
inequality for $\Eseq(\cdot)$ (Definition~2.5). While this inequality reflects
the classical inductive step in Harper's edge-isoperimetric theorem for Boolean
hypercubes \cite{harper1966optimal}, we formally mechanize it here to bound
arbitrary fiber partitions in $A(n,k)$.

\begin{lemma}[Subadditivity of A000788]\label{lem:subadditivity}
	For all $x, y \in \mathbb{N}$:
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:subadditivity}
		\Eseq(x) + \Eseq(y) + \min(x, y) \leq \Eseq(x + y).
	\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By strong induction on $x + y$: assume \eqref{eq:subadditivity} holds for all
pairs summing to less than $x + y$. Case-split on the parity of both $x$ and $y$
(four cases) and expand via the even/odd recurrences $\Eseq(2m) = 2\, \Eseq(m) +
m$ and $\Eseq(2m+1) = \Eseq(m) + \Eseq(m+1) + m$. In each case, the recurrences
halve the arguments, reducing the inequality to the inductive hypothesis at a
strictly smaller sum. Mechanically verified in Lean~4
(\texttt{E\_add\_min\_le}).
\end{proof}

For fiber sizes $c_1,\ldots,c_m$, write $y=|U_p(V')|$. The coordinate projection
is injective on each fiber, whose roots form a subset of $U_p(V')$, so $\max_i
c_i\le y$; iterating Lemma~\ref{lem:subadditivity} while tracking this maximum
yields $\sum_i \Eseq(c_i)+R-y\le\Eseq(R)$, the multi-way bound used below.
Consequently, the Hamming Ball maximizes the defect among all connected
subgraphs.

\begin{theorem}[Defect Bound]\label{thm:defect}
	For any $R$-element subset $V' \subseteq V(A(n,k))$:
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect-bound}
		D(V') \leq \Eseq(R).
	\end{equation}
	Equivalently, $U(V') \geq R \cdot k - \Eseq(R)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By strong induction on $R$. For $R \ge 2$, choose a coordinate $p$ at which two
vertices of $V'$ disagree and partition $V'$ into fibers $\{F_\alpha\}$ by the
symbol $\alpha$ at position $p$. Each fiber is strictly smaller than $V'$, so
the inductive hypothesis gives $D(F_\alpha) \leq \Eseq(|F_\alpha|)$. The
root-disjointness structure at coordinates $q \neq p$ yields the decomposition
$D(V') \leq \sum_\alpha D(F_\alpha) + R - y$, where $y = |U_p(V')|$. Applying
the generalized partition subadditivity (iterated Lemma~\ref{lem:subadditivity})
closes the induction. Mechanically verified in Lean~4
(\texttt{sum\_unique\_roots\_lower\_bound}).
\end{proof}

The exact lower-bound inequality below fails in general, including under its
embedding conditions.

\begin{proposition}[Refuted restricted Hamming-boundary inequality]\label{prop:restricted_lower_bound}
	Under the hypercube embedding conditions $k \ge \lceil \log_2 R \rceil$ and
	$n-k \ge \lceil \log_2 R \rceil$, every subset $V' \subseteq V(A(n,k))$ of
	cardinality $R$ satisfies
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:restricted-bound}
		\left|\extbd(V')\right| \ge \big(R \cdot k - \Eseq(R)\big) \cdot (n - k) - \Cconst(R).
	\end{equation}
The full Star in $A(11,6)$ at $R=31$ satisfies the embedding conditions but
has boundary $450<476$, so this statement is false even in its gated range.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the restricted bound]
The unrestricted form fails in $A(10,8)$ at $R=17$. More importantly, the
embedding conditions themselves are insufficient: the full Star in $A(11,6)$
at $R=31$ satisfies $\lceil\log_2 31\rceil=5\le6$ and $6+5\le11$, but has
boundary $450$ while the proposed lower bound is $476$. The full Star in
$A(12,6)$ at $R=37$ has slack $-57$ as well. Thus the embedding conditions
motivate the construction but are not a safe-range criterion.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Full-Star obstruction shoreline]
The formula-only Star diagnostic gives a sharper obstruction map without
constructing the factorial-sized arrangement graph.  For the radius-one Star
with $j$ active branches and $m=n-k$,
\begin{equation}

 X(S_j)=m(m-1)\frac{j(j-1)}{2}.
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}

\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Hamming Ball Evaluation]\label{prop:hb-eval}
	The lexicographic Hamming Ball $\mathrm{HB}_R$ achieves equality:
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:hb-eval}
		X(\mathrm{HB}_R) + \Eseq(R) = \Cconst(R).
	\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{lemma}[Deficit-compensated collision reduction]\label{lem:collision-reduction}
Let $m=n-k$, let $R=|V'|$, and write
$\delta_D=\Eseq(R)-D(V')$. Then Proposition~\ref{prop:restricted_lower_bound} for $V'$
is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deficit-compensated-collision}
X(V') \leq \Cconst(R)-\Eseq(R)+(m+1)\delta_D.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Substitute $U(V')=Rk-D(V')$ into \eqref{eq:boundary}, subtract the
right-hand side of \eqref{eq:restricted-bound}, and rearrange.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:collision-reduction} isolates the unresolved extremal
statement in a form that separates the intrinsic partial-layer allowance
$\Cconst(R)-\Eseq(R)$ from the additional allowance supplied by a defect
shortfall. It is an equivalent reformulation, not a proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:restricted_lower_bound}.

\begin{lemma}[Interface subadditivity]\label{lem:interface-subadditivity}
For pairwise-disjoint vertex sets $A,B,C$ in any loopless graph, where
$\partial X=N(X)\setminus X$, define
$\mathcal{L}(A,C)=|\partial A|+|\partial C|-|\partial(A\cup C)|$. Then
\[\mathcal{L}(A\cup B,C)\leq\mathcal{L}(A,C)+\mathcal{L}(B,C).\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The difference between the right- and left-hand sides is
\[
J=|\partial A|+|\partial B|+|\partial C|-|\partial(A\cup B)|
-|\partial(A\cup C)|-|\partial(B\cup C)|+|\partial(A\cup B\cup C)|.
\]
For a vertex outside $A\cup B\cup C$, its contribution is $abc$; for a
vertex in $A$, $B$, or $C$, it is respectively $bc$, $ac$, or $ab$, where
$a,b,c\in\{0,1\}$ record whether that vertex has a neighbor in the named
set. Every contribution is nonnegative.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Coordinate-local collision identity]\label{lem:local-collision}
Fix a coordinate $c$ and partition $S\subseteq V(A(n,k))$ into symbol slices $S_i$.
Regard each $S_i$ as its projection $Q_i$ in the local arrangement graph $A(n-1,k-1)$.
Let $L_c$ be the number of empty transversal slots which lie in a local boundary $\partial_{\mathrm{loc}}Q_i$.
Then
\[X(S)-\sum_i X(Q_i)=L_c.\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The global boundary $\partial S$ decomposes as the union of the lifted local boundaries and the empty transversal slots. The number of local boundary vertices that are also empty transversal slots is exactly $L_c$. By inclusion--exclusion, $|\partial S|=\sum_i|\partial_{\mathrm{loc}}Q_i| + \text{(empty transversal slots)} - L_c$.
The occupied-root count splits exactly as $U(S)=|\pi_c(S)|+\sum_i U(Q_i)$. Substituting these along with $|S|=\sum_i|Q_i|$ into the exact boundary formula \eqref{eq:boundary} cancels the local boundary terms entirely, leaving exactly $X(S)-\sum_i X(Q_i)=L_c$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Pool envelope]\label{lem:pool-envelope}
With the notation of Lemma~\ref{lem:local-collision}, write
$P_i=\{x\in\pi_c(S):i\notin x\}$ for the compatible projection pool at
slice $i$, and let $L_{c,i}$ denote the contribution of slice $i$ to
$L_c$ (the number of empty transversal slots in $P_i\setminus Q_i$ that
lie in $\partial_{\mathrm{loc}}Q_i$).  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pool-envelope}
	L_{c,i}\le|P_i|-R_i.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every empty transversal slot counted by $L_{c,i}$ is a projection
$x\in\pi_c(S)$ with $i\notin x$ (hence $x\in P_i$) that is not already
occupied by the slice $S_i$ (hence $x\notin Q_i$).  Therefore the
swallowed slots form a subset of $P_i\setminus Q_i$, giving
$L_{c,i}\le|P_i\setminus Q_i|=|P_i|-|Q_i|=|P_i|-R_i$.
\end{proof}

The remaining step to resolve a genuinely restricted version of this claim via
this route is a coverage-avoidance inequality bounding $L_c$. This inequality
remains open; the finite CP-SAT searches reported in Appendix~\ref{app:computational}
are diagnostic evidence only.

\begin{remark}
These propositions encode the extremal content of a vertex-isoperimetric
compression theorem applied to the arrangement graph fiber structure: Harper's
edge-isoperimetric theorem \cite{harper1966optimal} for the binary ($n=2$)
case, and the more general Bollob\'{a}s--Leader compression theorem for
vertex-isoperimetric inequalities on $n$-ary Hamming graphs
\cite{bollobas1991vertex} for $n > 2$. The Kruskal-Katona shadow theorem
\cite{kruskal1963number,katona1968theorem} is a related but distinct result
about uniform set systems (binary layers of the hypercube); it is not by
itself sufficient for the $n$-ary transfer required here (see the discussion
of this citation issue in the proof-sketch notes accompanying this project).
Proposition~\ref{prop:hb-eval} is
computationally verified through $R \le 160$ by constructing $\mathrm{HB}_R$ and
enumerating its boundary in $A(2R, R)$ (Appendix~\ref{app:engine}).
Proposition~\ref{prop:restricted_lower_bound} is tested exhaustively over connected
$R$-vertex subsets for $R \le 10$ by enumerating every non-isomorphic connected
$R$-vertex subgraph via the \texttt{nauty}-based engine (Section~3), and
separately over \emph{all} (including disconnected) $R$-vertex subsets, for
$R \le 6$ across 26 parameter rows of $A(n,k)$ (up to $3.24\times10^9$ subsets
in a single row), with no counterexample found in either search. Because
both searches are bounded in $R$ and in $m$, this remains only partial computational
evidence. The full-Star counterexamples show that the embedding condition
$R \le 2^m$ is not sufficient once $m \ge 5$; the restricted form is therefore
false in general, not an open hypothesis or an established safe-range theorem. The tested cases
with $m \le 4$ provide evidence only over the explicitly enumerated ranges.
Conceptually,
Section~\ref{sec:sandwich}'s asymptotic-dominance argument explains why, once
$n-k$ is large relative to the fixed cluster size $R$, no sub-optimal topology's
savings in cross-collisions can outweigh its linear-in-$(n-k)$ penalty for
missing defect --- an argument that holds regardless of $R$, but which by itself
does not establish a stronger exact lower bound at every finite scale; that
candidate is refuted by Proposition~\ref{prop:restricted_lower_bound}. The
remaining extremal classification problem and its methods are discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:funnel} and
Appendix~\ref{app:alt-strategies}. A further gap, independent of these
propositions, is the \emph{boundary-to-extraconnectivity reduction}: even with
an exact minimum-boundary theorem, one must still show that (i)~deleting the
Hamming ball's external boundary leaves every remaining component with at least
$R$~vertices, and (ii)~every minimum $(R{-}1)$-extra cut can be reduced to
isolating one connected component of exactly $R$~vertices---minimizing over
connected $R$-sets whose boundary deletion is itself a valid $(R{-}1)$-extra
cut---without increasing the cut. This reduction is not addressed in the Lean
formalization. The active Lean capstone proves the additive-error boundary
estimate and exact evaluation of the Hamming-ball candidate; it assumes no
global exact-boundary hypothesis. No extra-connectivity conclusion follows
without the separate component-size and converse-cut arguments.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Finite computer-assisted certificates]\label{prop:finite-certificates}
The certificate uses the following explicitly stated slice inequalities.  For a
coordinate split with $U$ occupied projections, defect $d=R-U$, and $s$
nonempty slices, its interface terms satisfy
\begin{align}
 \sum_c L_c &\le U\min(m,s-1)-d, \label{eq:line-slot-capacity}\\
 Q_m'(S_c)+L_c &\le m(k-1)|S_c|. \label{eq:local-capacity}
\end{align}
The first inequality is the line-slot capacity bound; the second is the local
boundary cap.  Since the same identity holds for every coordinate and
$\sum_p d_p=D(S)\le \Eseq(R)$, the per-coordinate relaxation is combined using
the upper concave envelope at average defect $\Eseq(R)/k$.  These are the
mathematical inputs to the C++ relaxation; they are not currently Lean-checked.

The C++ slice upper bound and exhaustive origin-pinned oracle establish the
following finite cases, subject to the stated slice inequalities and the
independent exhaustive checks. Every tested volume is certified for
$A(n,2)$ with $n\le14$. For $k=3$, the certificate covers every volume in
$A(5,3)$ except $R=5,6,7$, every volume in $A(6,3)$, $A(7,3)$, and $A(8,3)$
except $R=5$; the omitted rows are settled by exhaustive enumeration. In the
tested cases the exact values satisfy $Q_m(S)\le G_m(|S|)$, with equality in
the reported tight rows. These are finite computer-assisted results, not a
proof of the open restricted hypothesis.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[Finite unconditional instances]
For the finite graphs covered by Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-certificates},
the certified inequality together with the Hamming-ball evaluation gives the
exact minimum external boundary at every certified volume.  In particular,
the reported rows include all tested volumes of $A(n,2)$ for $3\le n\le14$,
all tested volumes of $A(6,3)$, $A(7,3)$, and $A(8,3)$, and the non-exceptional
volumes of $A(5,3)$; the omitted $R=5,6,7$ cases in $A(5,3)$ and $R=5$ cases
in the other $k=3$ rows are supplied by the exhaustive oracle.
This is a computer-assisted result, not a Lean theorem.
\end{corollary}

The unconditional boundary result is Theorem~\ref{thm:hamming-sandwich} in
Section~\ref{sec:hamming-sandwich}: it gives an additive-error lower bound
for every fixed-volume set and an exact Hamming-ball witness on the embedding
range. It asserts neither exact optimality nor a formula for $\kappa_g$.

\begin{remark}[Closed-form evaluation of $\Cconst$]
The summation $\sum_{i=1}^{R-1} \lceil \log_2(i+1) \rceil$ admits a closed form
by grouping indices by dimension level. For each $j \ge 1$, exactly $2^{j-1}$
indices satisfy $\lceil \log_2(i+1) \rceil = j$ (those with $2^{j-1} < i+1 \le
2^j$). Summing the complete levels $j = 1, \ldots, d-1$ via the identity
$\sum_{j=1}^{m} j \cdot 2^{j-1} = (m-1) 2^m + 1$ and adding the partial level at
$j = d$ yields
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:ceillog-sum}
		\sum_{i=1}^{R-1} \lceil \log_2(i+1) \rceil = R\,\lceil \log_2 R \rceil - 2^{\lceil \log_2 R \rceil} + 1,
	\end{equation}
	eliminating the last summation from the formula for $\Cconst(R)$.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Part~(i).} The lower bound is established directly by
Proposition~\ref{prop:restricted_lower_bound}.

\textbf{Part~(ii).} Proposition~\ref{prop:hb-eval} shows $X(\mathrm{HB}_R) +
\Eseq(R) = \Cconst(R)$. Substituting this into the boundary decomposition
equation~\eqref{eq:boundary} for $\mathrm{HB}_R$, where $U(\mathrm{HB}_R) = R
\cdot k - \Eseq(R)$, yields
	\begin{equation}
		\left|\extbd(\mathrm{HB}_R)\right| = \big(R \cdot k - \Eseq(R)\big) \cdot (n - k) - \Cconst(R),
	\end{equation}
meaning the lexicographic Hamming Ball has the displayed boundary. This
calculation is an exact evaluation of the candidate, not an optimality claim.
The embedding condition ensures $A(n,k)$ has a large enough alphabet and
enough positions to realize $\mathrm{HB}_R$ as an induced subgraph.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[System-Level Diagnosability]\label{cor:diagnosis}
Under the PMC and MM* models, the $g$-extra diagnosability $t_g(G)$ is derived
from the $g$-extra connectivity via $t_g(G) = \kappa_g(G) + g + 1$ when $|V(G)|
\ge 2g + 3$ \cite{cheng2023augmented}. The boundary sandwich does not determine
$\kappa_g$ and therefore gives no exact extra-diagnosability formula without
an additional boundary-to-connectivity theorem and the hypotheses of the
diagnosis model.
\end{corollary}
