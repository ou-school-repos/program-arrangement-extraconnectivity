\section{Introduction}

The arrangement graph $A(n,k)$, introduced by Day and Tripathi
\cite{day1992arrangement}, models interconnection networks for multiprocessor
systems. A vertex of $A(n,k)$ is an injective sequence of $k$ symbols drawn from
an alphabet of size $n$, and two vertices are adjacent if and only if they
differ in exactly one coordinate.

The $g$-extraconnectivity $\kappa_g(G)$ of a graph $G$ is the minimum number of
vertices whose removal disconnects $G$ into components each containing more than
$g$ vertices. The ``linearly many faults'' property, established for arrangement
graphs by Cheng, Lipt\'ak, and Yuan \cite{cheng2013linearly}, shows that under
sufficiently many faults the graph either remains connected or splits into one
large component with the remaining components totaling at most $g$ vertices---a
result that is tight only for small parameters and asymptotically tight
thereafter. To obtain \emph{exact} $(R{-}1)$-extraconnectivity one must reduce
the problem to minimizing the external boundary over connected $R$-element
subsets; this reduction is proposed here but remains open in full generality
(Section~\ref{sec:defect}). Cheng, Lipt\'ak, and Tian
\cite{cheng2022extraconnectivity} computed the corresponding small-$(g)$
extraconnectivity cases of $A(n,k)$ for $g \le 6$, with computer assistance;
this paper develops a conditional closed-form framework for all $R$, subject to
the two explicit propositions stated below. This vertex-fault metric serves as a
natural dual to the edge-fault matching preclusion metrics recently explored for
regular interconnection networks in \cite{cheng2025higher}.

Under the per-instance hypothesis~\ref{prop:restricted_lower_bound} (with the
Hamming-ball evaluation proved independently in~\ref{prop:hb-eval}), we show that for
all $R \ge 2$ with $n - k \ge \lceil \log_2 R \rceil$ and
$k \ge \lceil \log_2 R \rceil$, the minimum external boundary of any
$R$-element subset is bounded below by an explicit function of $R$, $k$, and
$(n-k)$, and that this bound is achieved by the lexicographic Hamming Ball.
The full
statement appears as Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

Our approach proceeds in three steps:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Failure of Geometric Compression (Section~\ref{sec:nogo}):} In
hypercubes, shifting a vertex to use a smaller symbol preserves the boundary. In
$A(n,k)$, the target symbol may already appear at another coordinate of the same
vertex, so the shift can either violate injectivity ($1324 \xrightarrow{4\to 1}
1321$) or sever defect links ($\{13,\,23,\,43\}$ form a clique along root $\_3$;
shifting $3\!\to\!1$ fixes $13$ and breaks two adjacencies).
\item \textbf{The Algebraic Defect Framework (Section~\ref{sec:defect}):}
Instead of moving vertices geometrically, we count the coordinate-aligned lines
(roots) that the subset touches. Each root extends to exactly $(n-k+1)$
vertices, so the boundary factors cleanly into a term proportional to $(n-k)$
minus a correction that depends only on the subset's internal defect and
cross-collisions---not on $n$ or $k$.
\item \textbf{The Fixed-Topology Asymptotic Penalty
(Section~\ref{sec:sandwich}):} For any fixed sub-optimal topology, the defect
shortfall contributes a linear-in-$(n-k)$ term while the remaining correction
stays topology-dependent but dimension-independent; consequently no sub-optimal
topology can catch up once $n-k$ is large enough.
\end{itemize}
The second and third of these are unconditional theorems, proved outright and
mechanized in Lean; only their combination into the single exact closed form of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, valid at every finite $R$ rather than merely in the
large-$(n-k)$ limit, depends on the two propositions introduced below.

The conditional exact boundary formula of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} has potential
consequences for the design and reliability analysis of fault-tolerant
distributed systems, pending closure of the two open propositions. In
multiprocessor interconnects and data center network architectures, the
closed-form boundary formula would provide system designers with the precise
redundancy requirements needed to guarantee that any $R$-node workgroup survives
catastrophic link failures, eliminating the wasted capacity inherent to looser
bounds. Furthermore, within distributed consensus protocols, this exact boundary
would quantify the minimum threshold of faulty nodes required to partition a
Byzantine fault-tolerant (BFT) quorum. Beyond these practical systems
applications, our Algebraic Defect Framework successfully extends Harper's
classical edge-isoperimetric machinery~\cite{harper1966optimal} into
permutation-constrained spaces, offering a new analytical baseline for
combinatorial optimization and coding theory on Cayley graphs.
