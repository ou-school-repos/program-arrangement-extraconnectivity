\section{Future ideas for Proving the Universal Boundary Inequality}\label{app:alt-strategies}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Everything in this appendix is a research sketch, not a result.
Nothing here is proven, checked, or claimed to be correct; it is recorded
only so that future work on Proposition~\ref{prop:universal} does not
restart from nothing. Neither strategy below has been carried past an
informal whiteboard stage.}
\end{quote}

The coordinate-root compression approach outlined in the accompanying
research notes (\texttt{docs/proof-\allowbreak sketch-\allowbreak weighted-potential.md}) stalls
on a coordinate-tangling obstacle: a local compression move at position
$p$ unavoidably scrambles roots at every other position $q \neq p$, and no
argument to date bounds that scatter damage tightly enough to recover
monotonicity of $X(V') + (m+1)D(V')$. Three alternative directions are
sketched below.

\paragraph{Marginal induction with partial-layer tracking.}
This direction extends the proof skeleton already used for
Theorem~\ref{thm:defect} (proven by strong induction on $R$, partitioning
$V'$ by a coordinate and applying Lemma~\ref{lem:subadditivity}), carrying
the joint quantity $\Phi = X + (m+1)D$ instead of $D$ alone. Stripping a
vertex $v$ with $s$ roots shared with the remaining $R-1$ vertices gives
$\Delta D = s$ exactly; closing the induction would require bounding
$\Delta X$ as a function of $s$ and $m$ alone, so that
$\Delta X + (m+1)s \le \Delta \Cconst(R) + m \cdot \Delta \Eseq(R)$. A
partial result toward this bound has since been derived (not yet
mechanized in Lean): writing out the marginal change in
$\mathrm{total\_coord\_edges}$ gives $\Delta X \le (k-s)m$ unconditionally,
and separately $\Delta X = 0$ whenever $v$ is adjacent to the vertex
removed (proved directly from the one-coordinate-difference property of
adjacency, independent of $n,k,R$). A second, dimension-independent cap
$\Delta X \le 2(R-1)$ also holds, from the fact that only vertices at
Hamming distance $2$ from $v$ can contribute to $\Delta X$ via a fresh
root. \textbf{This per-step marginal inequality is refuted.} An explicit
configuration in $A(5,3)$ ($k=3,m=2$, $R:2\to3$), with $D_{old}=0$
verified directly, achieves $\Delta X=2$, $s=1$, giving LHS $=5 >$
RHS $=4$: the per-step bound is false, not merely loose. The global
inequality still holds for that configuration only because surplus
banked at an earlier step compensates; see
\texttt{docs/proof-sketch-weighted-potential.md} for the full computation.
Consequently the appeal of this direction — reducing an $R$-vertex claim
to a single-vertex marginal check via strong induction — does not survive
as originally framed. A repair would need an amortized argument that
explicitly carries banked surplus between steps, which has not been
attempted and is not obviously simpler than the original claim.

\paragraph{Coordinate-partition induction on $\Phi$ (Strategy 3b).}
Distinct from the refuted marginal peeling above: Theorem~\ref{thm:defect}'s
actual proof never isolates a single vertex — it partitions all of $V'$
by symbol at one coordinate into fibers, recurses on each smaller fiber,
and bounds the recombination via Lemma~\ref{lem:subadditivity}. Whether
this skeleton closes for $\Phi=X+(m+1)D$ is untested territory distinct
from the peeling refutation above. A first attempt at verifying it
computed the recombination penalty by subtracting fiber totals from an
already-known $\Phi(V')$, which is tautological (true by construction for
any partition, independent of whether the induction actually closes). A
genuine geometric bound was then derived for the 2-fiber cross-collision
term: $X_{\text{cross}} \le \min\big(c_ac_b,\,(k-1)\min(c_a,c_b)\big)$ for
fibers of size $c_a,c_b$, which is independent of $k$ for fixed small
fiber sizes (an initial claim that this term grows unboundedly with $k$
was an error in combining two constraints additively rather than by
minimum). However, this bound is likely too loose at scale: the marginal
slack $\Delta\Cconst(R)+m\Delta\Eseq(R)$ for a single split is
$O(\log R)$, while $c_ac_b$ for a balanced split is $O(R^2)$ and even the
refined bound is $O(R)$ for fixed $k$ — numerically, at $R=20$ balanced
$(10,10)$ the slack is $6$ (at $m=1$) against bounds of $100$ and $40$.
Whether the true worst-case $X_{\text{cross}}$ (not these loose
combinatorial bounds) stays near $O(\log R)$ is unresolved. Two explicit
constructions at $k=3$ and $k=5$ have since been hand-verified: at $k=3$
($R=20$, balanced $(10,10)$ split), all $100$ candidate pairs collapse
onto only $20$ distinct collision targets, because $\mathrm{bd\_mult}$
counts distinct coordinates, not pairs, and $k=3$ leaves only one
non-partition coordinate for every pair to route through. A $k=5$
construction (matched pairs routed through $k-1=4$ \emph{different}
coordinates) avoids this collapse entirely, disproving collapse as a
general law — but it required $O(k)$ fresh alphabet symbols to keep each
pair's coordinate distinct, which forced $m$ large enough to supply them
in the one instance checked, and the resulting $X_{\text{cross}}$ still
sat comfortably under slack there. Whether this alphabet cost is a
necessary trade-off or an artifact of that one construction is itself
open; no construction achieving spread at small $m$ and large $k$ was
found in the search that produced these two examples. \textbf{The naive
per-split induction itself is refuted}, however: see below.

The natural next adversarial attempt --- maximizing $X_{\text{cross}}$
while keeping $m$ small and $k$ large --- turns out not to be an
independent test. Combining $\mathrm{total\_coord\_edges} = |\partial
V| + X$, $\mathrm{total\_coord\_edges} = U(m+1) - Rk$, and $D = Rk - U$
gives $\Phi = X + (m+1)D = mRk - |\partial V|$: the $U$ terms cancel
identically, so $\Phi \le \Cconst(R) + m\Eseq(R)$ is exactly
Proposition~\ref{prop:universal} restated, term for term. At fixed
$R,k,m$, maximizing $X$ \emph{is} minimizing $|\partial V|$ --- the
original problem, not a new adversarial target --- so a construction
with large $X_{\text{cross}}$ but also large boundary demonstrates
nothing. This is also the root cause of the tautological verification
noted above: any identity check on a $\Phi$-partition is true by
construction, since $\Phi$ carries no information beyond $|\partial
V|$. The non-vacuous form of the open question, stated directly in
boundary language: how many of one fiber's external neighbors are
absorbed (cease to be external) when the other fiber is unioned in.
That count is genuinely non-tautological and shares the same meaning
across all $(n,k)$; bounding it from fiber sizes and $m$ alone, rather
than bounding $X_{\text{cross}}$ directly, is what would close the
recombination step.

\textbf{The naive per-split induction is refuted}, on the same footing
as the marginal-peeling refutation above, by an explicit,
independently-verified counterexample. An exhaustive sweep of all
coordinate 2-way splits of every $R$-subset of $A(5,3)$ up to $R=4$
found $810$ splits (of $4{,}074{,}840$ checked) where the recombination
penalty $S$ exceeds the per-split slack $m\Delta\Eseq(R)+\Delta\Cconst(R)$.
The worst case, $V' = \{(0,1,2),(0,2,1),(0,3,4),(0,4,3)\}$ in $A(5,3)$
($n=5,k=3,m=2,R=4$) split into two disjoint ``swap pairs''
$F_a=\{(0,1,2),(0,2,1)\}$, $F_b=\{(0,3,4),(0,4,3)\}$, gives
$|\partial F_a|=|\partial F_b|=12$ with $D=X=0$ in each fiber, while
$|\partial V'|=16$, so $S = 12+12-16 = 8$ against a bound of
$m\cdot 2 + 2 = 6$ --- a deficit of $2$. Decomposing $S$ shows it is
\emph{entirely} cross-target collision ($X_{\text{cross}}=8$,
$\Delta D_{\text{cross}}=0$): the two fibers share $8$ common external
neighbors and neither fiber's boundary otherwise reaches into the
other. The global inequality still holds ($|\partial V'|=16 \ge 12$)
only because each fiber individually carries slack ($12-9=3$ each,
totaling $6$, absorbing the deficit of $2$ with room to spare) --- not
because the per-split bound holds. This is the direct analogue, for
the partition skeleton, of the marginal-peeling refutation: the naive
induction does not close step-by-step, and a repair would again require
an amortized argument banking slack across the recursion, which has not
been attempted for either skeleton.

\paragraph{Dual root-compression via averaging.}
Inspired by Pinto's resolution of the Bollob\'{a}s--Leader directed-path
conjectures on the hypercube \cite{pinto2015directed}, where two
compression operators that each individually fail to shrink a directed
boundary are shown to satisfy an averaging inequality forcing at least
one of them to be non-worsening. The single guarded-symbol compression
already refuted on $A(n,k)$ (Section~\ref{sec:nogo}) is one such
individually-failing operator; whether a dual pair of root-compression
operators exists on $A(n,k)$, respecting the injectivity constraint, for
which an analogous averaging bound holds for $X+(m+1)D$, is entirely
untried. This is flagged only as a structural analogy worth investigating
for the coordinate-tangling obstacle; no candidate operator pair has been
proposed.

\paragraph{A global Lyapunov function on fiber multisets.}
This direction proposes a potential $\Psi(V') = \sum_{p=1}^k \sum_r
f(|F_{p,r}|)$ over the multiset of fiber sizes at every coordinate, for
some convex $f$ --- a different functional from $\Phi=X+(m+1)D$ above,
not a renaming of it. Whether a single root-compression move's local
gain (from concentrating vertices into larger fibers at one coordinate)
always dominates its collateral loss (from scrambling roots at every
other coordinate) is the open question. The $\Phi$-collapse identity
above says nothing about $\Psi$: it shows $\Phi$ carries no information
beyond $|\partial V|$, but $\Psi$ is a genuinely different quantity, and
this direction remains untouched by that result. Neither the link
between $\Psi$ and $X+(m+1)D$, nor the required domination bound, has
been established; it is not known whether a suitable $f$ exists.

\medskip
\noindent None of these directions currently has a confirmed, closed
path to Proposition~\ref{prop:universal}. Both induction skeletons
tried are refuted in their naive, per-step form: marginal peeling's
per-vertex bound (Lemma~\ref{lem:subadditivity},
Theorem~\ref{thm:defect}'s induction skeleton, and the proved
$\Delta X=0$-at-adjacency lemma remain valid, but the induction built on
top does not close as planned), and coordinate-partition induction's
per-split recombination bound (an explicit, independently-verified
counterexample in $A(5,3)$ shows $S$ can exceed the per-split slack by
$2$, with the global inequality surviving only via slack banked in the
sub-fibers). Neither has an attempted amortized replacement. The
dual-compression direction's one tested candidate operator pair failed
its own averaging inequality and degenerated into a graph automorphism
on the example checked; no non-degenerate candidate has been proposed.
The Lyapunov direction ($\Psi$ over fiber-size multisets, distinct from
$\Phi$) remains a from-scratch invariant search with no candidate
potential shown to work. These are research opinions, not mathematical
claims, and should not be read as evidence toward
Proposition~\ref{prop:universal} itself; the exhaustive computational
sweep (Appendix~\ref{app:engine}) remains the only supporting evidence
for the proposition as of this writing.

\paragraph{Two further named directions, flagged at the same evidentiary
stage as dual-compression above.} Two classical tools were suggested
as possible replacements for the failed local decompositions: an
entropy/Shearer's-Lemma bound on the coordinate projections
$U_p(V')$, and a fractional linear-programming relaxation of the
isoperimetric problem with its dual. Neither has been written down for
$A(n,k)$, let alone checked against a single swept row, and both carry
a specific, identifiable risk rather than a generic one: $C(R)$ and
$E(R)$ are staircase functions of bit-length and popcount, and the
subadditivity lemma underlying \S\ref{sec:sandwich} is a carry identity
in binary addition, not a smooth or continuous relation --- a
continuous relaxation (LP duality) or a smooth bound (entropy) is not
guaranteed to recover an exact integer staircase, and may instead
certify only a nearby, weaker constant. Both natural candidates have
since been checked empirically and killed, in each case faster than the
hand algebra would have taken.

The most natural entropy candidate: taking $H_p(V')$ as the Shannon
entropy of the root-distribution (fiber sizes) at coordinate $p$ and
$S(V')=\sum_p H_p(V')$, the conjecture that the Hamming Ball minimizes
$S(V')$ --- the entropy analogue of Theorem~\ref{thm:defect} --- is
false already at $R=3$ in $A(5,3)$ (exhaustively checked over all
$487{,}635$ subsets at $R\le4$): the true minimizer
$\{(0,1,2),(0,1,3),(0,1,4)\}$ achieves $S=3.17$ against the Hamming
Ball's $S=3.42$, while having a \emph{larger} boundary ($12$ vs.\ $11$)
--- the functional does not merely miss the exact staircase, it
disagrees with the target in direction on the first case tested.

The natural LP relaxation fares worse: minimizing $\sum_w y_w$ subject
to $y_w \ge x_v - x_w$ for every edge, $\sum_v x_v = R$, $0\le x\le1$,
solved exactly (\texttt{scipy.optimize.linprog}, HiGHS) for $A(4,3)$
and $A(5,3)$, $R=2,3,4$, against the true integer-optimal boundary from
exhaustive search, gives LP optimum \emph{exactly zero in every case}
--- not a weaker constant but no information at all. The uniform
fractional point $x_v=R/N$ is always feasible and forces every edge
difference to zero, so $y\equiv0$ satisfies every constraint; the
relaxation has no mechanism forcing spread mass to generate boundary.
Consequently there is no dual certificate to examine, and the earlier
``banked slack looks like a dual certificate'' framing had nothing to
attach to.

Neither result rules out a more sophisticated version of either tool
(a different entropy functional; an LP with symmetry-breaking or
higher-order constraints), but no such candidate has been proposed.

\paragraph{The open problem, restated as a capacity/pigeonhole
aggregation bound (corrected).} An initial version of this paragraph
claimed only distance-2 pairs between $F_a$ and $F_b$ contribute shared
external neighbors, citing the $\Delta X=0$-at-adjacency lemma to
dismiss distance-1 pairs. That was a category error: the lemma concerns
a different setting (single-vertex marginal peeling, not two fixed
disjoint fibers) and is false here --- checked directly in $A(6,3)$,
$F_a=\{(0,1,2)\}$, $F_b=\{(3,1,2)\}$ (distance-1, sharing a root at
$p=0$) gives $|\partial F_a\cap\partial F_b|=2=m-1$, nonzero.

The corrected picture has two channels. \textbf{Channel 1 (distance-1,
root-sharing), genuinely $m$-linear:} if $u\in F_a,v\in F_b$ share a
root at $p$, every extension not already used is a shared external
neighbor: exactly $m+1-|\{u_p,v_p\}|=m-1$ of them (generally
$m+1-|A_r\cup B_r|$ for larger co-occupying groups). This is the only
channel whose count is literally linear in $m$; it vanishes correctly
at $m=1$. \textbf{Channel 2 (distance-2, midpoint), gated but
$m$-free per pair:} for $u,v$ differing at $p$ and one other coordinate
$q$, the candidate $w$ (swapping $u$'s value at $p$ to $\beta=v_p$) is
valid \emph{iff} $\beta\ne u_q$, and the mirrored candidate iff
$\alpha\ne v_q$ --- \emph{two independent conditions}, verified
directly: $A(5,3)$, $u=(0,1,3)$, $v=(1,2,3)$ has $\beta=1=u_q$ (first
candidate invalid) but $\alpha=0\ne v_q=2$ (second valid), giving
exactly one shared neighbor, not zero or two. $m$ does not enter this
per-pair condition.

Finally, the aggregation is not pair-disjoint: distinct pairs can
generate the same target, the same $k=3$ collapse mechanism established
elsewhere in this appendix, so the per-pair sum over channel 2 is only
an upper bound, not an exact count. The open problem, correctly scoped:
bound $|\partial F_a\cap\partial F_b|$ from $(c_a,c_b,m)$ alone, as the
union of both channels adjusted for their mutual overcounting --- not
simply ``sum a 0--2 count over distance-2 pairs,'' which both
undercounts (omitting channel 1) and overcounts (ignoring collisions)
simultaneously. No such bound has been derived or attempted. $m$ enters
through two distinct, now-identified mechanisms: root-sharing capacity
(channel 1) and cross-pair symbol supply for spreading (channel 2, via
the unproven collapse/spread trade-off); a correct bound must account
for both and their interaction. This remains the cleanest surviving
open question after this appendix's testing, and its own first draft
undercounting the problem's difficulty is worth remembering when
reading the next attempt at it too.
