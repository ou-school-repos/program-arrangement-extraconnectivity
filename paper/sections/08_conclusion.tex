\section{Conclusion and Open Problems}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The recursive, single-coordinate Star is an obstruction family whose boundary
comparison is controlled by $(m,j)$.  At $A(10,5)$ with
$R = 26$ we exhibit an exact discrete counterexample: a $5$-dimensional binary
hypercube slice attains boundary $\SliceBoundary{}$, strictly beating the
canonical star's $\StarBoundary{}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:crossover}).  The
lexicographic increment trace (Figure~\ref{fig:trace}) couples the crossover to
a mechanism: the star confines its internal adjacencies to isolated coordinate
fibers, accumulating boundary debt before loops can close, whereas the
orthogonal slice distributes variation across all $k$ coordinates and closes
loops early.  The same geometric friction defeats strictly local averaged
squeezes on star closures --- the exact slack deficit $-8$ on the full Star
$S_4 \subset A(8,4)$ --- so the mechanism is not an artifact of $A(10,5)$
alone.

By encoding the exact set-theoretic isoperimetric search as a Boolean
constraint program over the $30{,}240$ vertices of $A(10,5)$
(Appendix~\ref{app:computational}), we certify a global floor
$|\extbd(S)| \ge \CertFloor{}$ for every volume-$26$ subset under a
vertex-transitivity symmetry pin, bracketing the true optimum as
$\CertFloor{} \le |\extbd(S)| \le \SliceBoundary{}$.  The search window below
the slice --- the target of $\HuntTarget{}$ --- remains open: we hold no
feasible witness at or below it, only the certified floor, which is a moving
record that advances as the core-guided search continues.

The unconditional boundary result is the Hamming sandwich in
Section~\ref{sec:hamming-sandwich}. It follows from the root-count estimate and
collision charging and is formalized in Lean. The embedding condition ensures
that the Hamming-ball comparison set exists; it does not imply optimality.
For example, the full Star in $A(11,6)$ with $R=31$ satisfies the embedding
conditions but has boundary $450$, below the Hamming value $476$. The finite
crossover results are instances of the broader unresolved profile problem,
not a universal safe-range criterion. The boundary profile alone does not
determine extra-connectivity without a separate cut/component reduction.

\textbf{Future work.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Closing the computational interval.} The immediate target is to
resolve the window $[\CertFloor{}, \SliceBoundary{}]$.  The certified floor was
reached via core-guided search that archive over a million learned clauses;
integrating graph-specific symmetry breaking into the branching heuristics and
deploying distributed solving should push the floor toward the slice, or
discover an incumbent at or below $\HuntTarget{}$.
\item \textbf{Mapping the shoreline.} It remains to characterize the exact
$(m,j)$ region in which a full Star beats or loses to the Hamming-ball value,
and to determine whether other topology families produce earlier obstructions.
\item \textbf{Interconnection networks.} The arrangement graph is a highly
symmetric topology used in parallel computing.  The shift from hierarchical to
distributed optimal packings as the alphabet scales bears directly on fault
tolerance, bisection bandwidth, and routing in $A(n,k)$; quantifying the
crossover's effect on these metrics is the applied follow-up.
\end{itemize}
